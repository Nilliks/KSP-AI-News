    Gene, mission planning: Short answer: no — not as flown, but close if you fly it efficiently. You need roughly 3,400 m/s surface-to-80 km orbit and your actual total is 6,402 m/s, so on paper there's plenty. The catch is you're at 15.6 km already with only 463 m/s and apoapsis 26.9 km — your current trajectory is going to come back down, and Kerbin's atmosphere eats a lot. The two big stages (stage 4 at ~3,057 m/s and stage 2 at ~2,572 m/s) carry almost all of it; stages 6, 5 and 7 are small altitude boosters.
    Linus, flight dynamics: What worries me more than the delta-v total: your orbit has periapsis at -599 km, eccentricity 0.998 — you're essentially on a ballistic arc, and 2,269 m/s is what a circularization at apoapsis would cost right now.
